## Theory of leadership

**Servant leadership** — defined personally as:
> *Trailblazing new paths of societal improvement that others can continue. Serving others most by creating opportunities for them to reach their full potential.*

Operating principles, distilled:

1. **Train people in skills you lack — don't clone yourself.** The ASME design team grew because I integrated officers strong in what I wasn't.
2. **Value to members > growth of the organization.** The first ASME launch failed because I led with the org. The second succeeded because I led with the value to people.
3. **After every event, change something.** Sustained intensity even when things are working. Iterate weekly.
4. **Remain a student of those you want to teach.** For someone to listen fully, they need to first feel seen and heard. Humility is operational, not decorative.
5. **Train your replacement before you need to.** Both ASME and DM kept growing after I left because the next leaders started several steps ahead.
6. **Show your mistakes openly.** Vulnerability with the team enabled the team to teach me, which was crucial for my own growth.

## Penn State ASME — Founder of design team, then chapter president

### Phase 1 (Sophomore year — failure)
Founded the design team. First semester: founding partners ghosted. 3D-printed RC car project made no meaningful progress. Failure was the foundation for the rebuild.

### Phase 2 (Sophomore → junior — rebuild and stabilize)
- New branding, new structure, new team, improved strategy.
- Recruited 5 officers; grew to 20 consistent members.
- Built the first multifaceted engineering design team at Penn State — *space where students with big ideas that didn't fit elsewhere could connect and create*.
- Sustained intensity: every event reviewed; team structure changed weekly.
- 2 semesters in: 40 active members, 2 projects, stable.

### Phase 3 (Junior — expansion to college-of-engineering scope)
- I wasn't running the projects anymore — the people I trained were.
- Joined **Penn State Engineering Diversity Roundtable**.
- Created a CAD-skills pipeline with **NSBE** for non-technical-club students to add to portfolios.
- Co-organized the **2025 Penn State Equity Summit** (100+ students, staff, faculty, administrators).
- Penn State School of Engineering Design adopted **Autodesk Fusion** as the official software for the introductory design course (700 students/year). The team's "CAD nights" became part of the course's official syllabus.

### Phase 4 (Senior — president of the parent organization)
- Ran the **18-person ASME executive board**.
- Maintained oversight of the now **10-person design team board**.
- Went from coordinating 40-person events to running 100-person events.
- Launched a **corporate sponsorship program** that raised thousands of dollars.
- **60+ events** in two semesters.
- Rebuilt the **28-person leadership structure**.
- ASME chapter membership grew **40 → 120**. Design team peaked at **80 members and 4 projects**.

### Phase 5 (post-graduation legacy)
The new leadership team continues the culture. The design community keeps growing without me.

## Penn State DiscipleMakers — Treasurer → President

### Why this organization mattered
DM is a community where students explore their beliefs and articulate their perspectives through conferences, retreats, workshops, and discussions. The word *disciple* is often translated as *apprentice*, and the notion of approaching life with the humility and curiosity of an apprentice resonated.

### Phase 1 (Sophomore — joined and led a discussion group)
Joined first year, led a philosophical discussion group in second year.

### Phase 2 (Junior — treasurer, observation, reading, conversations)
Joined exec board as treasurer. Two semesters of observation, reading, conversations. Critical lesson: *to lead the culture change I envisioned, I had to remain a student of those I wanted to teach.*

### Phase 3 (Senior — president, plan execution)
- 100+ active members across 1 large group + 10 small groups, 25 leaders, 5-person exec board.
- **Three-part plan: change culture, equip leaders, demonstrate practicality.**
- **Equip leaders:** worked with external staff to rebuild the leadership training program; personally ran multiple workshops and meetings.
- **Demonstrate practicality:** iterated my own discussion group. Average group size was 6. Mine grew to **20+**. Members started independently modeling the welcoming culture for the rest of DM.
- **Two keynote speeches** to 80+ students and staff (had not been done by a student member in several years).
- New small-group model now part of DM.

### Phase 4 (Recognition + handoff)
Received the **Bold Leader Award** at graduation. New leadership continues the culture.

## Why the leadership story matters for the research story

> *Managing demanding extracurriculars while building a research portfolio was often challenging, and the tenacity forged during that journey gives me the endurance to execute my current research plan.* — GRFP personal statement

The skills transfer:
- **Multi-year arcs.** ASME took 4 years to mature. DM took 3. The PhD plan is 5. Same patience.
- **Failure as data.** First ASME launch failed → second succeeded. First boxing tryout failed → made the team. Same approach applies when an experiment fails.
- **Team composition.** Integrate strengths I lack. The interdisciplinary lab structure (Co-Design Lab + B-Crew HCI + Autodesk Research) is built on the same principle.
- **Servant + self-pushing balance.** The thread through ASME (serve), DM (serve), and boxing (push self) is the same one running through the dissertation: build open-source tools that serve future engineers, while pushing personal capability into AI, HCI, and computational design.
