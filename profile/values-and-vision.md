# Values & Vision

The internal compass. What I believe, where I'm trying to go, and the principles that I hold even when no one is watching. Not for fellowship-essay paste-in. This is for re-reading when the work feels off-direction.

---

## Core values, in priority order

### 1. Service over status
Inspired by both parents — decorated NASA engineering careers, both of whom turned down higher-paying jobs for public-interest work. *Research projects that were uncertain and nonlucrative.* That orientation is the load-bearing one.

Operationally:
- Open-source as a default, not an afterthought.
- Tools designed for people with fewer resources, not just for tomorrow's experts.
- "Create technology that lasts longer than my name and my generation."

### 2. Tenacity (intense and persistent diligence)
The personal motto. The Penn State *I Engineer* word. Operationalized as:
- Multi-year arcs over single-semester wins.
- Show up to the conditioning test even when you've already been cut.
- After every event, change something.
- "Sustained intensity even when doing well — push like it was day one."

### 3. Action-grounded curiosity and creativity
Curiosity is only valuable when it leads to exploration. Creativity is a skill, cultivated by exposure and iteration. Applied creativity = *carefully crafting ideas, boldly implementing them, reimagining them without becoming fixated.*

### 4. Servant leadership
Not being in charge — benefitting those in your charge. Trailblaze paths others can continue. Train the people you'll hand off to. Show your mistakes openly so others can teach you back.

### 5. Apprenticeship as a stance
The DM "disciple = apprentice" framing applied generally. Approach life with the humility and curiosity of an apprentice. Remain a student of those you want to teach.

### 6. Intellectual honesty about positioning
*"I am still a mechanical engineer, not a programmer."* Know where the contribution sits. Don't pretend to be the AI researcher when you're the ME researcher who uses AI.

---

## What "impact" means

Three concentric circles:

1. **The work outlives me.** ASME design team and DM kept growing after I left. The PhD output should outlast the dissertation.
2. **Capability transfers.** People I trained went on to do better work than I did. The dissertation's open-source models should let government, national labs, and engineers without expensive degrees do better work than I did.
3. **The next generation has more options.** Specifically: engineers from underrepresented backgrounds, people without access to expensive equipment, machinists transitioning to monitoring multiple AI agents instead of operating one machine at a time.

## Career trajectory — what I'm explicitly *not* committing to

> *Whether I will be a professor, an entrepreneur, or a project manager is still unknown.*

What's known:
- Aim to be a **multidisciplinary leader who trailblazes innovation in the design and manufacturing of complex parts and systems.**
- Bring together experts in **cognitive science, AI, user-centered design, manufacturing, and business** to develop financially-sustainable but accessible tools.
- Likely shape: **running a lab that develops open-source engineering tools** OR **leading an R&D division that improves the government's capability to develop new products.**
- Less likely: pure-startup pursuit of profit. Possible: mission-driven startup whose moat is the lab partnership.

## Lines I won't cross

- I won't shortcut public-interest framing for higher-paying private opportunities — even when offered.
- I won't oversell what I can do. The Hertz line — *"I am still a mechanical engineer, not a programmer"* — is honest framing, not false modesty.
- I won't take credit for work done by people I trained. The CV / fellowship narratives are explicit about *"I did not design these parts; I created the project overall."*
- I won't drop the boxing record into technical talks unprompted. Save it for character moments.
- I won't make the religious affiliation a wedge in professional contexts. Reference it for what it taught about leadership, then move on.

## The line that closes most personal statements

If the work isn't on track to do that, course-correct the work. Don't soften the line.
