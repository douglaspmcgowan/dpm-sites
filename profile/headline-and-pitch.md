# Headline & Elevator Pitches

Voice notes:
- Lead with the *what* (research / problem), not the credentials.
- "I engineer tenacity" is the personal motto — keep it for moments when character is the question, not the work.
- Avoid the word "passionate." Use "spent the last X years on…" instead.

---

## LinkedIn / one-line headlines

Pick the one that fits the audience:

- **Research forward** — *PhD student at the UC Berkeley Co-Design Lab — building AI co-creation tools for manufacturing-aware design.*
- **Industry forward** — *PhD researcher in AI-driven design-for-manufacturing | Previously Rivian, Penn State ARL, Autodesk*
- **Mission forward** — *Making manufacturable design faster, cheaper, and more accessible — at UC Berkeley with Autodesk Research.*
- **DoD/government forward** — *Mechanical Engineering PhD researcher | Trusted AI for manufacturing-aware design | Previous Secret clearance*

## 2-minute pitch (panel intro, podcast intro)

> I'm Doug McGowan. I'm a first-year mechanical engineering PhD student at UC Berkeley, in the Co-Design Lab — Kosa Goucher-Lambert's group, which sits at the intersection of design cognition, computation, and human–computer collaboration.
>
> The problem I'm working on: when you build something complex — a satellite, a car door, a defense system — the most expensive moment is when the designer and the manufacturer disagree, late in the process, about what's actually buildable. There's a study from the Institute for Defense Analyses showing that low-Earth-orbit systems take an average of seven years from idea to spacecraft, and a big piece of that is the design-to-manufacturing bottleneck. My thesis develops what I call a co-creation framework — designers, manufacturers, and AI working concurrently in CAD/CAM throughout early design, instead of CAM being an afterthought. The first aim is empirical: study how designers and machinists actually communicate, and where they break down. Aim two collects training data through digital design aids — machinability rubrics, qualitative-feedback tools, geometry-assessment tools. Aim three trains agentic AI models on that data and tests them in real workflows. We're doing this with the Autodesk Industry Futures team and the Berkeley Institute of Design's makerspace.
>
> The personal context: I came to Berkeley from Penn State — 4.0 in Mechanical Engineering, Schreyer Honors, with research at Purdue's Zucrow Labs on a DARPA propellant project and at Penn State's Applied Research Lab on composite–steel bolted-joint dynamics. I interned at Rivian on side closures, where my single-piece die-cast door redesign supported a patent application. Outside research I founded Penn State's first multifaceted ASME design team — grew it to 80 members across four projects — ran the Penn State ASME chapter as president, led DiscipleMakers, and competed on the D1 boxing team. I retired 3-0 with the Coaches' Choice award.
>
> The thread through all of it is what Penn State filmed me saying for their *I Engineer* series: I engineer tenacity. The five-year research plan is going to require it.

## Anti-patterns

These have been flagged or self-corrected — don't reuse:

- ❌ "I'm passionate about manufacturing." — too generic, says nothing.
- ❌ "I do AI for engineering." — too broad; specify *manufacturing-aware design* or *design-for-manufacturing*.
- ❌ "I'm a first-year, so I'm still figuring out my research direction." — you have a five-year plan; lead with it.
- ❌ Calling the boxing thing first when the audience is technical. Save it for character moments.
- ❌ "Diversity-focused" without context — say *first multifaceted engineering design team at Penn State* and let the work speak.
