# Fellowship talking points — keyed by audience

For each major fellowship: what reviewers actually care about, the framing to lean into, the framing to avoid, and the lines from existing drafts that landed best.

---

## NSF GRFP

**Reviewers care about:** *Intellectual Merit* and *Broader Impacts*, scored separately and weighted equally. The 3-page personal statement and 2-page research plan are read by 3 panelists who are not necessarily in your subfield.

### Framing to lean into
- **Intellectual merit:** the dissertation's three-aim plan reads like NSF — empirical study, then methodology development, then model training. State the research questions explicitly (the GRFP draft already does this).
- **Broader impacts:** open-source tools, dataset release, accessibility for engineers without expensive degrees, NSBE pipeline at Penn State, design-team founding, Equity Summit.
- **Track record of completing what you start.** ASME design team scaling 0 → 80, ASME chapter 40 → 120, DM presidency, Coaches' Choice. Multi-year arcs.

### Framing to avoid
- DoD/national-security language. NSF is a science-funding agency; *defense* and *warfighter* don't help here. Save those for NDSEG.
- Overclaiming on AI/ML expertise. Reviewers from CS will see through it; reviewers from ME may discount it. The "ME who applies AI" framing is honest and correct.

### Lines that work (from drafts)
- *"I aim to create technology that lasts longer than my name and generation, impacting society for decades to come."*
- *"There is already an abundance of intelligent computational systems with incredible potential to make physical things that remains underutilized. I intend to realize the potential of these existing tools."*
- *"My value system is inspired by my parents, who both have decorated engineering careers at NASA. They worked for public interest, not profit."*

## NDSEG (DoD National Defense Science & Engineering Graduate Fellowship)

**Reviewers care about:** Scientific/engineering merit, alignment with DoD priorities, and a credible plan to deliver value to the DoD.

### Framing to lean into
- **DoD critical-technology areas:** *Trusted AI* and *Human-Machine Interfaces* — both are explicit hits.
- **Acquisition bottleneck framing.** GAO and IDA reports on long DoD acquisition timelines. Manufacturing-aware design as a wedge.
- **Open-source for cross-agency implementation.** ERDC ITL-1 / ITL-2 alignment.
- **Workforce transition narrative.** The aging machinist workforce → next generation monitoring multiple agents.
- **Maintainability / reparability** for mission-critical systems — when a crucial part is needed, future warfighters don't need to wait on the original manufacturer.
- **Past clearance.** Interim Secret during ARL role — credibility cue for clearance-eligible work.

### Framing to avoid
- Pure-academic framing without operational impact.
- Hedge language about whether the work matters for defense. State it directly.

### Lines that work (from NDSEG draft)
- *"As the demand for fast development of complex vehicles, weapons, and information systems increases, my research enables the DoD workforce to maintain a competitive edge."*
- *"Manufacturing-aware design enables the rapid development of bespoke defense systems, increasing adaptability on and off the battlefield."*
- *"The 'valleys of death' between the idea and the physical solution can be bridged more efficiently through manufacturing-aware design."*

## Hertz Foundation Fellowship

**Reviewers care about:** Outlier potential. They want people who will reshape a field. Less interested in checkbox achievements; very interested in originality, intensity, depth of conviction, and a credible vision.

### Framing to lean into
- **Tenacity as the defining word.** The Penn State *I Engineer* video tagline. Reviewers respond to a person who has clearly defined themselves.
- **Multi-year arcs.** ASME (2.5 years to 80 members from a failed launch). DM (3 years from joiner to president). Boxing (2 years from cut walk-on to undefeated). All three demonstrate the same pattern.
- **The "applied curiosity" and "applied creativity" framing.** It's distinctive and authentic.
- **Combat sport.** Hertz reviewers value depth of character. The boxing arc lands here in a way it wouldn't elsewhere.
- **Vision statement explicitly avoiding overcommitment.** *"Whether I will be a professor, an entrepreneur or a project manager is still unknown."* Hertz wants ambition without false certainty.

### Framing to avoid
- Reading like a CV in paragraph form. Hertz wants the person, not the resume.
- Performing humility. They want confidence with self-awareness.

### Lines that work (from Hertz long statement)
- *"I engineer tenacity."*
- *"My perspective on curiosity and creativity is centered around action."*
- *"For me, applied creativity involves carefully crafting ideas, boldly implementing them, and reimagining them without becoming fixated."*
- *"My servant leadership is not limited to my professional career; it extends to my community as well."*
- *"For me, boxing was never needed to be fun. It was supposed to be a forge."*
- *"Normally, engineers pull back from sports as classes get harder — perhaps the safer choice. I didn't, and now I'm at UC Berkeley on a full one-year fellowship."*

## DoD SMART Scholarship

**Reviewers care about:** Stem talent that will commit to government service after graduation. Service obligation is real (typically year-for-year).

### Framing to lean into
- Mission alignment with DoD agencies (AFRL, ARL, NRL, ERDC).
- Past clearance + ARL experience as proof of fit.
- Concrete sponsorship-agency match — the NDSEG proposal's ERDC framing applies here too.

### Framing to avoid
- Suggesting you're using SMART to fund the PhD without commitment to the service obligation. Reviewers spot this.

**Reviewers care about:** Diversity of the academy, commitment to the diversity / equity / inclusion of higher education. Strong scholarship and demonstrated track record of contributing to equity.
